# Analysis-on-Real-Estate-Data-across-the-United-States

The U.S. government maintains thousands of buildings across the country, and effective management of these properties
requires understanding their geographical distribution, historical significance, accessibility compliance, and infrastructure gaps.
This project analyses the “Real Estate Across the United States (REXUS) Inventory (Building)” dataset 
to derive actionable insights for better infrastructure planning, maintenance, and accessibility improvements.

# Objectives

    •	Clean and preprocess the dataset to handle missing values, duplicates, standardize text, and parse dates.
    •	Analyse the geographic distribution of buildings by state, city, and county.
    •	Assess building ownership (Owned vs Leased) across states.
    •	Evaluate the compliance of buildings with ABA accessibility standards.
    •	Analyse the age of buildings and their historical designations.
    •	Identify and visualize buildings with no parking space.
    •	Explore and visualize the distribution of historical properties across different states.
    •	Predict whether a government building is Owned or Leased based on its location, age, usage, parking availability, and compliance attributes.
    •	Predict Architectural Barriers Act (ABA) accessibility standards and influencing factors.
    •	Estimate the age of a building based on its structural attributes, location, and functional characteristics.
    •	Predict the number of parking spaces available in a building based on its size, age, and usage.

# Summary of Results

    •	Classification models effectively identified ownership, historical status, accessibility compliance, and parking availability.
    •	Tree-based models consistently outperformed linear models for non-linear relationships.
    •	Regression models showed moderate performance, highlighting data limitations.
    •	Feature importance analysis confirmed that building age, size, and infrastructure attributes are the most influential factors.
    •	Identified regions with a high concentration of government-owned or leased buildings.
    •	The compliance rates for ABA accessibility were mapped, allowing for easy identification of areas with high or low compliance.
    •	Visualized the distribution of historical properties, offering insights into the cultural heritage of different states.
    •	The project demonstrates effective application of predictive analytics on a real-world government real estate dataset. By integrating data preprocessing, exploratory analysis, machine learning models, and interpretability techniques, the study provides valuable insights into ownership patterns, compliance risks, and infrastructure capacity.
    •	The findings can support policymakers and facility managers in prioritizing renovations, improving accessibility compliance, and optimizing asset utilization across federal buildings.
